How to write a film review

A simple structure for a fair review without spoiling the ending.

A review tells another person whether a film is worth their time. It is your opinion plus a few facts.

Start with the title, the year, and one sentence about the kind of story. Say it is an adventure, a comedy, or a quiet family film. Do not retell every scene.

Describe what worked. Maybe the main character had a clear goal. Maybe the music matched the boat scene. Use examples from the beginning or middle, not the ending.

Describe what did not work, in a fair way. A slow start is a real note. An insult about the actors is not a review.

End with who might like it. A film can be right for a family night and wrong for someone who wants a fast chase. That ending helps the reader more than a score alone.

Keep the review in your own words. Copying a newspaper review is not your review.
