Acting basics for a school film

Simple habits that make a line easier to believe.

Acting in a school film means saying the line as the character, not as yourself reading a page. The audience should understand what the character wants.

Learn the line well enough to look up from the page. Eyes that stay on a script make the scene feel unread. A cue card beside the camera is a fair help.

Stand still at the start of the line unless the story needs a move. Extra shifting pulls attention from the words. Speak a little slower than normal conversation. The microphone is closer than a friend across a table.

Listen to the other actor. A reply looks real when it follows the line just spoken. If you start talking before they finish, the edit becomes harder.

A mistake is a reason for another take, not a reason to stop the project. Mark the good take so the editor can find it.
