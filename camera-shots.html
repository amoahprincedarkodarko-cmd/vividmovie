Camera shots explained

Wide shots, close shots, and why a film changes the picture size.

A shot is one uninterrupted picture from the camera. Films change shots so the audience knows where to look.

A wide shot shows the whole room or the whole street. It tells the viewer where the scene happens. A family film often opens with a wide shot of a house, a school, or a boat.

A medium shot shows a person from the waist up. It is useful for a conversation. A close shot shows the face. Filmmakers use it when a feeling matters more than the room.

The camera can stay still, follow a person, or look down from above. A still camera is easier for a first film. Movement is useful only when it helps the story.

Before you film, sketch three shots: where the scene is, who is talking, and one important object. Three planned shots are clearer than one long, shaky recording.
