What a director does

A family guide to the person who guides the actors and the camera.

The director is the person who decides how a story should look and feel. The script gives the words. The director turns those words into scenes people can watch.

Before filming, the director talks with the writer, the camera lead, and the actors. They walk through the room, or the drawn set, and choose where each person stands. This plan keeps the crew from guessing on the day of the shoot.

On set, the director asks for another take when a line is unclear or a move happens too fast. A take is one filmed try. A short scene can need many takes. The director picks the one that tells the story best.

The director also works with the editor after filming. They watch the pieces in order and decide what stays. A joke that was funny on set may slow the story down. Cutting it can make the film clearer.

In a school film, one student can be the director. That student does not have to operate the camera. Their job is to keep the story clear for the audience.
