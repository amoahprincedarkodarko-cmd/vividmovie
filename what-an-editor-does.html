What an editor does

How the filmed pieces become one story.

The editor takes the filmed takes and builds the order the audience will see. The job starts after the camera work, and it can change the meaning of a scene.

First the editor watches the usable takes and marks the clear lines. A take with a loud truck in the background may lose to a quieter take, even if the actor preferred the louder one.

Then the editor joins the shots. A wide shot can introduce a room. A closer shot can show the reply. If both shots stay too long, the scene drags. If they change too fast, the audience gets lost.

Editors also set the pace. A comedy often cuts on the reaction. A calm story holds the shot so the room can be seen. Music and sound effects are placed so they do not cover the words.

In a class project, the editor can be a different student from the director. They should agree on the story shape before cutting, then watch the cut together once without stopping.
