How animation works

Animation is a movie made from pictures that change a little at a time. When the pictures play quickly, they look like movement.

In drawn animation, artists make many pictures of the same character. One picture may move a hand. The next picture moves it a little more. A feature film needs thousands of drawings.

Stop-motion uses real objects. A puppet is moved a tiny bit, then photographed. The Adventures of Prince Achmed, from 1926, used paper cutouts and is one of the oldest surviving animated features.

Computer animation builds characters as models. Animators pose the model, and the computer draws the frames. The story still starts with a script and a storyboard.

An animated film you make for school can be shared by you. An animated film from a studio stays with that studio unless it is in the public domain or you have permission.
