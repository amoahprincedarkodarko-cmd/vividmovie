Sound in movies

Why voices, footsteps, and quiet moments are recorded on purpose.

Sound is half of a film. A clear voice can save a simple picture. A noisy room can hide the story even if the picture is bright.

Dialogue is the words people say. Record it close to the speaker, away from a fan or a busy road. If the voice is hard to hear, the audience stops following the plot.

Sound effects are the noises that match the action: a door, a bicycle, rain. Some are recorded on the day. Others are added later so they are clean.

Music can signal that a scene is happy, tense, or finished. Use music you made, music in the public domain, or music with permission. A song from a phone playlist is usually not free to put in a film.

Silence is also a choice. A quiet moment before a line can make that line easier to notice.
