Costumes and sets

How clothes and rooms tell the audience where a story happens.

Costume is the clothing a character wears. A set is the place the scene is filmed. Both tell the audience facts before anyone speaks.

A school uniform, a raincoat, or a paper crown can show a role. Costumes do not need to be expensive. They need to be consistent. If a character wears a red scarf in one scene, the scarf should return in the next scene unless the story explains the change.

A set can be a real room or a corner with a sheet on the wall. Remove posters and bottles that do not belong in the story. Extra objects pull the eye away from the actors.

Light matters in a small set. A window can be the main light. A lamp from the side can keep faces from looking flat. Avoid filming straight into a bright window unless the story needs a silhouette.

Write a short list before filming: three costume pieces and three set pieces. That list keeps a weekend project from becoming a search for random props.
