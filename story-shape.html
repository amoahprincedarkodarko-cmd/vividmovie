The shape of a story

Beginning, problem, and ending for a short film.

A short film needs a shape the audience can follow. A useful shape has a beginning, a problem, and an ending.

The beginning introduces a person and a place. One sentence is enough: a student misses the bus, or a robot finds a lost glove. Do not explain the whole life of the character.

The problem is the reason the film exists. The character tries something, and it does not work the first time. A second try can fail too. The tries should be visible, not only talked about.

The ending shows the result. The student walks, the glove is returned, or the character learns to ask for help. An ending can be happy or simply clear. It should answer the problem you opened with.

If you can tell the film in three sentences, the shape is ready to film. If you need a long explanation, cut the extra events before you shoot.
