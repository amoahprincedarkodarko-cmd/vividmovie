How film ratings work

What family, guidance, and age labels mean before you press play.

A rating is a label that helps a family decide if a film fits the people in the room. Different countries use different letters, but the idea is the same.

A family label means the film was made for a wide age range. A guidance label means a parent may want to watch first or watch with a child. An older label means the film was made for teenagers or adults.

Ratings look at scary moments, rude language, and dangerous behavior. They are a guide, not a full review. A family can still choose a different film if a scene is too intense for someone in the room.

A website should not hide the label. If VividMovie describes a film, the description stays general and does not retell the intense parts.

For a school screening, ask a teacher which label is allowed before the day of the showing. That question prevents a last-minute change.
